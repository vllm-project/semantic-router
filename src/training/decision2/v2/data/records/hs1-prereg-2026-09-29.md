## 0. Independence rules (binding for every HS1 file)

- **No evaluation material.** HS1 never uses any of the following, whether as items, paraphrases, templates, seeds,
  names or designs:
  - JevBench items or family names;
  - JevArena v3 typed FINAL or human-transfer items;
  - typed-FINAL or typed-DEV generators or draws (`benchmark/generate.py`, including `resource_ledger`, per the 16:40
    rule);
  - dev-panel items (typed DEV, SELECT, CAL/CAL698, CSS pilot, HT-DEV and its 14 sources, Score5-DEV, score5t-dev,
    mlx-diag);
  - C1 sources or source extracts;
  - HotpotQA, including the quarantined 4B near-match group.
- **Scope of the generator code.** It imports nothing from `benchmark/`, `jev_arena/`, `v2/eval/` or panel files. It
  reuses only the data track's verified helpers: row contract `training/model/data.py`, option builders
  `v2/data/sources/common.py`, and name and date helpers `v2/data/verifiable/core.py`.
- **All text is project-authored.** It is rendered from templates written in the HS1 generator source, with no
  third-party corpus text. **No LLM writes, paraphrases or naturalises any HS1 text in v1** (0 GPU-h for text). A
  naturalised variant would need a new preregistration or amendment.
- **Gold comes only from structure.** Each row is generated from a structured world (facts, rules, dates, amounts).
  A deterministic solver computes its label, and an independent re-check (§4) confirms it. No model output is used for
  a label, a filter or a selection. The one exception is the validity check in §7, which is diagnostic and never
  changes rows.
- **Adjacent skills, disclosed.**
  - F2's precedence and exception reasoning and F3's condition gating are the same abstract skills as typed FINAL
    `exception_stack` and typed DEV `rule_precedence` / `attribute_gate`.
  - HS1 uses none of their generators, schemas or items. Its states are English prose, not short dicts.
  - Formal gains on those typed families after HS1 training should be read as possible skill transfer, and the data
    card says so.

## 1. Families and generator designs

Every row is one native decision through the house renderer (`training/model/decision_model.segments`).

- **State:** an English prose string.
- **Instructions:** the question.
- **Options:**
  - Choice uses positional keys `o1..oK` from `choice_options`, with the gold position balanced.
  - Noul uses `noul_options("en")`.
  - Score uses `score_options` with keys `0..K-1` ascending and one criterion per level.
- **Identifiers:**
  - row ids `hs1-<family>-<sha24>`;
  - `group_id` `hs1:<family>:<sha24>`, one per generated world;
  - `source` `decision2_hardskills_hs1`;
  - `render_template` `hs1/<family>/<kind>/<variant>`.
- **`audit_metadata`:** generator, version, kind or domain, subtype (mechanism or trap), `facts_sha256`,
  `oracle_label`, `recheck_label`, seed group, the length class, and the quoted answer where one exists.

### F1 `hs1_quote_check`: quoted-conclusion verification

- **World.** Each world has:
  - a kind: the evidence structure, a solver and error mechanisms;
  - evidence records rendered as prose (a short note, or a long thread or report with distractor records);
  - one question and its solver answer.
- **The quote.** A named person with a role gives a conclusion and a one-to-two-step rationale: a note, draft
  recommendation, review comment, chat message or email.
  - Its position (top, middle or end), channel, role and hedging are drawn independently of correctness.
- **Two rows per world, one group.** They share the evidence and the question and differ only in the quote:
  - **right:** the conclusion and rationale follow the solver;
  - **wrong:** they follow one error mechanism, whose output differs from the solver's.
  - The wrong rationale cites true evidence and one wrongly derived or superseded value. The quote templates are the
    same for both, so the quote text alone cannot predict correctness.
- **Error mechanisms** (defined by HS1, generic reasoning slips; each kind implements 3–4):
  - `overlooked_condition`: a failing requirement is ignored;
  - `stale_value`: a value that a later update superseded;
  - `arithmetic_slip`: an omitted or double-counted item, or an inclusive/exclusive day count;
  - `entity_swap`: an attribute of a similarly named record;
  - `short_chain`: a lookup chain stopped one link early or followed through the wrong link;
  - `boundary_misread`: "at least" vs "more than", or a unit slip;
  - `scope_misapplied`: a rule applied to a category it doesn't cover.
- **Kinds.** Six kinds are used in TRAIN and dev-id; two appear only in the dev-ood slice.

  | Kind | Evidence | Mechanisms (subset) | Slice |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `order_sla` | dispatch/delivery times, promised windows, business days, reroute updates | stale, arithmetic (day counting), entity swap, boundary | train, dev-id |
  | `expense_total` | line items, per-category caps, tax, approval thresholds | overlooked cap, arithmetic, boundary, scope | train, dev-id |
  | `applicant_screen` | requirement list and applicant files | overlooked condition, entity swap, boundary, expired credential (stale) | train, dev-id |
  | `project_timeline` | tasks, durations, dependencies, working days, revised estimates | stale, arithmetic (sequential vs parallel), short chain | train, dev-id |
  | `directory_route` | people, teams, cost centres, budget owners, delegations | short chain, entity swap, stale (expired delegation) | train, dev-id |
  | `rubric_pick` | weighted criteria, proposal scores, a mandatory disqualifier | arithmetic (unweighted sum), overlooked disqualifier, boundary | train, dev-id |
  | `transit_connection` | legs, local times, time zones, minimum connection times | arithmetic (zone sign), boundary, stale timetable change | dev-ood only |
  | `subscription_bill` | plan changes mid-cycle, proration, credits | arithmetic (proration), stale price, scope | dev-ood only |

- **Interfaces** (drawn per world; a kind that cannot express one redistributes its share):
  - **Choice, 40%:** the options are the candidate answers. They always include the gold and the wrong quote's
    answer, plus other mechanism outputs. The quoted option in the right row is the gold.
  - **Noul-direct, 25%:** the underlying yes/no question. Gold is constant within the world and balanced across
    worlds, and one quote asserts the opposite of gold.
  - **Noul-verify, 20%:** "Is X's conclusion correct?" The right row is yes and the wrong row is no.
  - **Score, 15%:** an ordinal outcome with 3–5 levels. The wrong quote asserts a level 1–2 away from gold.
- **Length.** 75% of worlds are short: 600–2,000 characters of state. 25% are long: 3,000–7,500 characters, with the
  relevant records mixed among distractor records and template filler.

### F2 `hs1_policy_packet`: long policy packets

- **Packet.** One domain policy document of 4,000–12,000 characters (median target about 5,500). It contains:
  - a title and edition line; definitions; a scope; base provisions;
  - 1–4 amendments with effective dates, some superseded by a later amendment and some not yet in force at the case
    date;
  - optional regional or category annexes, temporary measures with in-force windows, and "notwithstanding" exceptions;
  - an explicit interpretation and precedence section, stating one of three precedence schemes per packet;
  - distractor sections: procedures, records, contacts, questions and answers, unrelated definitions.
- **Case.** A request or claim with 4–7 attributes and a decision date. The case text is appended to the packet in the
  state.
- **Engine.** Each outcome variable (an amount limit, a required approver, eligibility or coverage, a fee band) is set
  by the highest-precedence provision that applies under the packet's stated scheme. A provision applies when it is
  in force on the relevant date, the case is in its scope and its conditions hold.
  - Within one provision class, the latest in-force amendment replaces earlier text.
  - The solver records the full applicability trace.
- **Traps** (each row has 1–4; the trace records which of them decided the answer):
  - `amendment_in_force`;
  - `amendment_not_yet_in_force`;
  - `superseded_amendment`;
  - `exception_applies`;
  - `exception_condition_unmet`;
  - `annex_in_scope` / `annex_out_of_scope`;
  - `temporary_measure_expired`;
  - `cross_reference`: a definition in another section decides a condition.
  - Naive heuristics that ignore precedence are designed to be right on at most about 40% of rows. These are: the
    base text only, the latest amendment regardless of date, and the first matching provision. Being right some of
    the time keeps "avoid the base value" from being a shortcut.
- **Two cases per packet, one group.** They are different cases on the same packet, drawn so that their golds differ
  where the outcome space allows.
- **Domains.** Eight are used in TRAIN and dev-id: `travel_expense`, `returns_refund`, `leave_policy`,
  `library_lending`, `it_access`, `grant_funding`, `parking_permits`, `warranty_service`. Two are dev-ood only:
  `tuition_refund`, `venue_booking`.
- **Interfaces:**
  - **Choice, 45%:** the outcome value among 4–5 options, namely the gold plus naive-heuristic outputs and one
    plausible other value.
  - **Noul, 35%:** is the request within policy / eligible / covered.
  - **Score, 20%:** a band or tier with 3–5 levels.

### F3 `hs1_unmet_condition`: "condition not met" Noul negatives with matched positives

- **World.** It has:
  - a rule text with 3–6 required conditions, of which 0–2 are "either … or" alternatives;
  - 0–3 optional or recommended items, which are not required;
  - a case narrative;
  - the question "does it qualify / can it proceed / is it allowed".
- **Requirement kinds:**
  - `minimum` / `maximum` thresholds;
  - `date_window`;
  - `before_deadline`;
  - `document_provided`;
  - `signer_role`, where near-miss roles such as a deputy or an acting role count as not met unless the rule says so;
  - `category_match`;
  - `count_at_least`;
  - `same_party`: the account or holder must be the applicant;
  - `valid_on_date`: the credential must be valid on the event date.
- **Two rows per world, one group.**
  - **Negative:** exactly one required condition fails (85%) or two fail (15%). Gold is no, or a failing-condition
    option.
  - **Matched positive twin:** the same world with the failing attribute moved just inside the rule. Gold is yes, or
    "all met".
  - Near-boundary values and negated statements about optional items occur in both twins at equal rates, so negation
    words, boundary proximity and length do not predict the label.
- **Domains.** Eight are used in TRAIN and dev-id: `grant_application`, `rental_application`, `equipment_loan`,
  `travel_grant`, `club_membership`, `event_permit`, `scholarship`, `volunteer_clearance`. Two are dev-ood only:
  `claim_filing`, `vehicle_inspection`.
- **Interfaces:**
  - **Noul, 70%.**
  - **Choice, 20%.** It is one of two forms. In "which requirement is not met", the options are the requirement names
    plus "all requirements are met". In "which applicant meets every requirement", there are 3–4 applicants, exactly
    one meets everything in the positive twin, and in the negative twin none meets everything, so "none of them" is
    offered.
  - **Score, 10%.** A compliance grade: 0 means a required condition fails; 1–3 are graded by recommended items. The
    twin pair is level 0 vs a level of 1 or more, and levels are balanced across worlds.
- **Length.** 70% of states are 400–1,200 characters and 30% are 1,200–3,000.

## 3. Splits and the held-out development slices

- **Seed-group isolation.** TRAIN worlds are generated from the seed namespace `hs1-train:<family>:<k>`. Development
  worlds come from a disjoint namespace, `hs1-dev:<family>:<k>`. No world, packet, case or group appears in both.
- **dev-id** (two thirds of each dev slice) uses TRAIN kinds and domains with fresh seed groups. It measures the skill
  on familiar surfaces.
- **dev-ood** (one third) uses only the held-out kinds and domains above, which have no TRAIN rows. It measures
  transfer beyond the TRAIN templates. These kinds and domains share only the generic quote, packet and case
  scaffolding with TRAIN.
- **Row fields.** Dev rows carry `split=select` and `evaluation_role=select`. There is no separate AHO slice: the dev
  slices are the held-out material.
- **Uses of the dev slices.**
  - They are exported as the development panel **`hs1-dev`**: gold-free `{id, state, questions:{decision:…}}` prompts
    plus a gold file in the `v2.eval.sealed.build.gold_record` format, with task = family and a long flag. The export
    round-trips through `training/model/infer.question_to_row`.
  - Tracks may read `hs1-dev` in development to watch the three skills. **Nobody trains on it.** Selection is allowed
    under the usual development rules, never on public 231.

## 4. Gold verification

- **Oracle vs re-check, 100% agreement required.** Each generator has a solver on the structured world (the oracle)
  and an independent re-check:
  - F1 and F3: a second solver written separately, run on facts re-parsed from the rendered state by per-template
    patterns;
  - F2: a second, brute-force evaluator of every provision's applicability from the recorded provision set.
  - Any disagreement stops the build.
- **Presence check.** Every fact the solver uses must appear verbatim in the rendered state. Every fact rendered as a
  distractor must be non-decisive: re-solving without it gives the same label.
- **F1:** 100% of wrong-quote answers differ from gold, and 100% of right-quote answers equal gold.
- **F3:** 100% of twins differ only in the designated attribute or attributes, and the label flips as designed.
- **Unit tests** cover each kind, domain and trap on hand-checked worlds.

## 7. Validity check (cheap; diagnostic only)

- **Panel registration.** `hs1-dev` becomes a `DEVELOPMENT` entry in `v2/eval/panels.py` with hashes, plus
  `PANEL_ROLES` in `v2/eval/leak_audit.py` and a test. This is a small, separate shared-module commit, called out in
  the report.
- **Models, at the formal runtime settings, both on node A GPU1** (shared lease `owner.data-hardskills`), through the
  frozen runner `v2/eval/run_same_panel.sh` in image `decision20-train-fast:host2` (`f83b1d10…`):
  - **DEV2.0-4B:** release package `llm-semantic-router/DEV2.0-4B@452f1332…`, adapter spec
    `dev2-dec-package-t1.json`, T = 1, 16,384-token limit, with a fresh copy of the N4XF node-A autotune cache.
  - **Decider 4B:** `Mapika/decider-4b@eb5fbdfc…`, adapter `decider`, its published `system_one` settings.
  - A 20-item smoke run per model goes in a separate run directory first.
- **Metrics:**
  - per-family accuracy, pooled over interfaces, with invalid or missing answers counting as wrong;
  - per interface and per dev-id / dev-ood;
  - F1 adopt rate, the share of answers equal to the quoted answer;
  - F3 false-yes rate on negatives.
- **VALID rule (fixed now).** A family is valid if Decider 4B − DEV2.0-4B accuracy has a paired cluster-bootstrap 95%
  CI lower bound > 0. Clusters are `group_id`, with 10,000 resamples and seed 20260929. The rule is applied
  separately to F1 and to F2, on the whole dev slice.
  - The slices are valid if both F1 and F2 pass, mirroring the public-231 gap.
  - F3 is reported, not gated. The expectation is DEV2.0-4B false-yes ≥ Decider's.
- **Budget: ≤ 0.3 GPU-h in total, including smokes and any amendment rerun.** No other HS1 GPU use.
- **If a family fails:**
  - It is still published, flagged "dev slice not validated", with a diagnosis.
  - At most **one** amendment is allowed. It must be committed before new rows, change the generator for a stated
    reason, and use a fresh dev seed namespace `hs1-dev2:`. The rerun uses the same models, within the same 0.3 GPU-h.
  - The dev slice is never tuned on model outputs beyond that single recorded amendment.
- **No selection.** The check selects nothing and no track selects on it. Public-231 hard is read only in formal runs,
  as the out-of-sample check for models trained on HS1.

## 8. Stop rules and deviations

- A failed A1, a failed preflight or an unfixable audit failure stops the step. It is recorded, not retried to fill
  time.
- Any change to the designs, sizes, audits or the validity rule above is a committed amendment, made before the step
  it changes.
- The first build's statistics may show that a design target is infeasible, for example an interface share for one
  kind. In that case the build redistributes within the family, and the actual numbers are recorded; that is not an
  amendment.

## 9. Adoption by the size tracks (recommendation; each track decides in its own preregistration)

- **Where HS1 goes.** Add HS1 TRAIN as a separate block in the next milestone. A running milestone may add it only by
  committed amendment. Suggested dose: all 20k rows (about 8M tokens, ~4–6% of an XL-r2-sized mixture), with a
  matched-token control that removes the same number of tokens from the existing mix.
- **What to read.** `hs1-dev` is the development readout, reported per family and per dev-id / dev-ood. Public-231
  hard (long policy and planted-conclusion subsets) is the formal out-of-sample check under successor rule item 7.
- **HS1 rows carry gold labels only.** Teacher targets on them (own 1.0 or others) would need a separate
  preregistration.
