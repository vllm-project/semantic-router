# Jev ecosystem audit for Decision 2.0

**Source and date.** The [All about Jev](https://hanxiao.io/all-about-jev/) site reported 1,691 entries when opened on 2026-09-26 UTC. Its [JSONL download](https://hanxiao.io/all-about-jev/all-methods.jsonl), fetched at 12:39 UTC, contained **1,690 valid rows** and had SHA-256 `612332c1ac1209554c054afb60b2bdfb4854064dcb927a470a1096c9304ba2c5`. This appears to be a live-index timing difference; the audit uses the downloaded bytes. Within them are **200 model, 169 benchmark and 54 paper rows**. The [423-row machine-readable inventory](jev-catalog-inventory-2026-09-26.jsonl) screens every row and distinguishes a catalog claim from a pinned primary artifact. The source's prose is not republished. This audit checked the original abstracts for all 54 papers, pinned 23 key model cards and 14 reachable benchmark repositories, and read the methods or results of selected primary sources. It has not independently run every catalog model or validated every benchmark label.

**Interpretation of priority.** `P0` means directly useful for an exact unified comparison or research decision; `P1` warrants focused follow-up; `P2` is adjacent or unverified; `P3` is an index, landing page or commentary. Priority is not a performance rating. Scores in `catalog_score_claim_unverified` are the source's claims and must never be copied into a Decision 2.0 score table as our measurements.

### Native execution details that affect comparability

- [Eikos 4B](https://huggingface.co/caiovicentino1/Eikos-4B/tree/582ffb13f19a4da3f455e3db198584190bd7755b) and [27B](https://huggingface.co/caiovicentino1/Eikos-27B/tree/e3ce53a27c2bdb26450d5c26d79695808c32de0b) each ship MIT `decision_core.py`, `letter_adapter.py`, `serve.py` and `serve_vllm.sh` with weights. Their documented native path runs the vLLM engine plus `serve.py` for `/v1/systemone`; the cards require **vLLM ≥0.30.0** for hybrid Gated DeltaNet batching correctness. The code maps A–Z plus one-token two-letter labels and can use a tournament above the one-pass limit. Use native outputs and pin the `decision_config.json` maximum; a generic text-generation answer is a different readout. The 27B card says it is a rank-64 LoRA fine-tune of Qwen3.8-27B; the 4B card describes soft teacher probabilities, order permutations, EN↔PT consistency and model-soup selection. Both author evaluations are public/development evidence.
- [Decider 35B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Mapika/decider-35b-a3b/tree/91470e65478656eb68fde8c0ccdfe575d69d0309) loads via `from decider.infer import Decider; d = Decider("Mapika/decider-35b-a3b", use_graphs=False)`; it also ships `decider.serve` for `/v1/systemone`. Its card specifies 2–255 options, 32k state, **at least one 80 GB GPU**, about 65 GB BF16 weights, `torch>=2.14`, `transformers>=5.17` and `flash-linear-attention`; the older eager expert loop can be much slower. Do not compare its latency against dense models without preserving this implementation and hardware context.
- [APUS 4B runtime](https://huggingface.co/apus-ailab/APUS-OpenJev-v1-4B/blob/422b3741f8b5c092eeefef847c1ca89d78337d45/RUNTIME.md), [9B runtime](https://huggingface.co/apus-ailab/APUS-OpenJev-v1-9B/blob/82c9c56cfa9de8d36704ed91948d4726ef111635/RUNTIME.md) and [35B runtime](https://huggingface.co/apus-ailab/APUS-OpenJev-v1-35B-A3B/blob/090388abe2237117cc3b4df6f4250e81cbb36864/RUNTIME.md) specify `hf download ... --revision <commit>`, installed pinned requirements, then `python examples.py . --device cuda:0 --effort high`. The library entrypoint is `from openjet_runtime import OpenJet; model = OpenJet.from_pretrained("."); model.decide(request, effort="high")`. The Qwen3.5 4B/9B checkpoints run 16 low or 32 high layers; 35B runs 20 low or 40 high. The reference runtime requires Transformers 5.16.1 and CUDA PyTorch 2.8, single-request PyTorch execution, maximum 8,192 input tokens, and candidate IDs/descriptions. `score_level` is one binary proposition, not the full ordinal Score type. The 35B card estimates ≥75 GB BF16 memory and reports validation on a 96 GB GPU, not a proven minimum. All three require new probability calibration after merging: the 35B source-adapter comparison shows up to 0.149/0.178 absolute probability drift for high/low even though merged runtime self-check passed. Its candidate probabilities are relative preferences, not calibrated correctness probabilities.
- [APUS training provenance](https://huggingface.co/apus-ailab/APUS-OpenJev-v1-4B/blob/422b3741f8b5c092eeefef847c1ca89d78337d45/training.md) describes a 5,949-record curriculum over 3,898 parent groups, low/high CE plus high-to-low KL, and a 4B pilot-adapter initialization. The 9B card selects checkpoint-3000 rather than automatically calling checkpoint-5949 best. The schedule mixes Mind2Web, HelpSteer3, SGD, GoEmotions, BoolQ, MNLI and local counterfactuals; raw datasets and a complete rights clearance are not included in the weight repo. [Frozen80](https://huggingface.co/datasets/apus-ailab/APUS-OpenJev-Eval-Frozen80) is only 80 repeatedly used development questions. Distillation from the model's full depth into its shallow exit is an ablation idea; its author numbers do not establish general transfer.

## Benchmark and paper evidence that changes the protocol

1. [JevBench v1.4.2 method and results](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/JevBench/blob/main/docs/RELEASE-v1.4.2.md) rank Decider-4B v2 at 64.13 composite against Jev 1.13 at 63.29, while Jev leads in the **Intelligence** axis 53.1 to 49.4. Its aggregate mixes calibration, speed and cost; public and sealed item distributions differ. Publish each axis and the full denominators. Treat exposed items as development checks and do not turn the composite into an accuracy claim.
2. [Decision Bench](https://github.com/atlanai/decision-bench) freezes 1,071 public examples from 35 tasks/11 use cases and includes source catalogs, labels and hash checks. It is useful as a common real-data regression view but cannot serve as a new blind test. [Decision Models Under Pressure](https://github.com/gazelle93/decision-models-under-pressure) explicitly audits label-only and position shortcuts while varying candidate count, order and near distractors. Its `gates_passed=false` because the text-blind gold picker exceeds its threshold in six of ten cells; use paired diagnostic deltas rather than the absolute score as a headline. The [fixed-source pressure panel protocol](pressure-panel-protocol-2026-09-26.md) gives SHA-pinned data, licensing, same-question construction, common 2–16 capacity and scoring. [Open JevBench](https://github.com/model-collapse/jev-bench) separates gold from silver labels and all three answer types; do not pool those reliability tiers.
3. [Computational-social-science transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24574v2) is a strong **human-label** anchor: 7,977 items across 18 tasks; Jev trails the best LLM on 14 of 15 evaluation tasks and has a median 11.6 macro-F1-point gap. It reports high-confidence near-chance errors on an empathy task. This supports the frozen CSS pilot/evaluation split, task macro-F1, Brier/NLL, group calibration and coverage-risk curves. Existing public test sets may have pretraining exposure; disclose that separately from exact training overlap checks.
4. [Option-name/rubric reversal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26758v2) shows that schema validity can coexist with answer inversion, and reports 4.1 times fewer flips for a span-pooled comparison family than a constrained name-token readout. Train and test explicit option-name swaps, polarity reversals, order permutations, neutral labels and rubric bindings. Compare a candidate-conditioned semantic head with letter-logit controls at fixed backbone, training data and compute.
5. [JevOut](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30243) reports adaptive context flips on initially correct decisions; [Decision Hijacking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28613) finds probability shifts under injection with lower validated target success. Measure clean accuracy, fixed-budget targeted flip rate, confidence of wrong choices and natural distraction separately. The attacker-feedback protocol is not an ordinary zero-shot accuracy metric.
6. [Same Scores, Different Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27678) varies hypothesis visibility, requested outputs and order on ContractNLI: repeated agreement alone can hide stable wrong answers. [Jev for Scientific Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24965v2) separates semantic selection from deterministic downstream quantities and final labels. Add consistency and consequence checks, including when a final label stays correct for the wrong reason.
7. [Pinocchio v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24881v2) finds Jev reaches 0.666 AUROC on 1,718 text responses for *external answer-correctness prediction*. This is a different task and is not a direct Decision-vs-Jev typed-choice result. Its useful lesson is to assemble hard positive and negative correctness examples, grouped by original question, and measure discrimination as well as calibration. The paper's Jev appendix is in **v2**, not v1.
8. [This-That](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23886) reports that additional training on five known synthetic families failed to transfer to 13 others. [Decider-4B](https://huggingface.co/Mapika/decider-4b) reports that hard-label replay sharpened probabilities and hurt sampled behavior, then soft replay restored much of it. Together they favor new source families, matched-token ablations, old-model soft replay, and development gates that protect old tasks. These are research hypotheses for our architecture; they are not a published Jev training recipe.
9. [Calibration OOD probe](https://github.com/scienthoon/jev-ood-calibration), [Eikos's calibration section](https://huggingface.co/caiovicentino1/Eikos-4B), and our CAL observation all point to the same risk: a temperature learned on easy examples can be actively harmful on hard target distributions. Keep Choice/Noul/Score and answer-count bands distinct, report pre/post calibration metrics, and permit T=1 when independent calibration does not transport. [Classic temperature scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599) supplies the method, not a transport guarantee.

## Implications for Decision 2.0 training and release

- **Training:** Compare (A) current candidate-conditioned head, (B) token-logit readout, and (C) mean-pooled option/rubric readout with a frozen common backbone/data/budget where feasible. Introduce adversarial rubric bindings, boundary cases, multi-hop rules and realistic abstention cases from *new* families. Keep soft replay and token-/type-/language-matched controls to separate new-data gains from exposure and forgetting.
- **Calibration:** Fit only after checkpoint selection on a separate difficult, balanced CAL. Measure transportation to both synthetic and human-label development panels. Report per-type Brier, NLL, ECE, risk/coverage and invalid/truncation, before and after fit. Do not interpret a lower ECE on one panel as a universal gain.
- **Evaluation:** Retain the existing frozen family-disjoint synthetic final and human-label CSS 15-task final; add a distinct public regression panel and paired robustness battery. Compare exact model revisions under identical typed inputs, and show each model's native admission and projection policy. Separate quality, calibration, speed and cost. Family-level and task-level lower tails matter more than one weighted overall score.
- **Release:** The collection may be **Decision 2.0** while model repositories use `dev-2.0-xxb`; each card should disclose architecture, exact sources/licenses, freeze/selection/calibration/final hashes, self-contained runtime, size-specific 2.0 vs 1.0 and open-model rows, task matrix, rank graphic and any failed release gate. Published 1.0 scores remain historical development context.

The companion JSON files in `source/` pin the card and repository revisions and their README/card hashes. They verify artifact identity and accessibility, **not** author performance claims. The raw catalog snapshot remains local because the derived audit needs only factual metadata and attribution.
