## P0: Authentic ordinal Score and rubric conventions

[Rao and Callison-Burch](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29769v1) compared Jev's Choice-over-ordered-levels with native Score on seven human-rated graded panels. Native Score had higher within-one-level accuracy on all seven, and the two framings differed significantly on four; the paper attributes much of the difference to returned probabilities rather than only decoding. It also found shared downward level offsets across Jev and three LLM judges. The authors **did not test** the proposed cause: raters may have followed population norms or scale conventions absent from the judge-visible rubric. Their rubric versions also differed from the original rater instructions, so the study cannot prove a model architecture defect.

Our frozen human-transfer panel projects 15 tasks to Choice, while Decision Bench v4 is Choice-only. The 400 synthetic Score decisions and current authored pilots test explicit rules and arithmetic, not agreement with independent human ordinal ratings under a complete rubric. A model can therefore improve JevArena's transfer axis and still fail subjective ordinal Score. The current 27B Score pilot addresses explicit level selection, but its result cannot close this different gap.

**Prospective DEV experiment:** curate a small *source-disjoint* human-rated ordinal panel with each unit's original rater instructions and level anchors. Audit rights and source IDs against all training/selection/calibration sets; document unavoidable pretraining-exposure uncertainty. Freeze rubric, labels and review protocol before inference. Where both native primitives exist, run each model twice on the same units and level text: native Score and Choice over those ordered level descriptions, with an explicit, identical policy for “not assessable”; record unsupported coverage otherwise. Do not silently add that option only to Choice: the paper did, so its framing difference is not a pure head ablation. Report exact and within-one accuracy, quadratic weighted kappa, signed level offset, level-use histogram, ordinal probability quality, invalid answers and unit-clustered intervals. Independently test a *fully specified* rubric against a faithful short version on separate paired units; a change in gold meaning invalidates the pair. For training, compare semantic level/rubric conditioning with a letter readout at fixed backbone, source groups, token budget and adapter; retain the untouched base. Stop if gains fail on both human ordinal DEV and existing typed/CSS DEV. This diagnostic stays outside the already frozen six-axis release scalar until a future preregistered arena version.

## P0: Shared-state request shape and co-question interference

[TypeSafe's model reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models) says Jev evaluates many questions against one state, with a 64k whole-request budget and a separate 32k `state + longest question` limit. Its [jaggedness guide](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13) warns that irrelevant state and indirection reduce accuracy. An independent [benchmark repository](https://github.com/yodablocks/jev-orderby-bench) measured a much narrower application: on the same 360 human-labelled rows, putting 40 rows into one state changed 77 binary decisions versus its one-row request shape and increased inversion. The repository attributes much of the damage to position and shared-state length; this is **not** evidence that our model or ordinary multi-question fan-out changes by the same amount.

Current panels exercise both one-question records and shared multi-question records, but do not isolate request packing while keeping the target question and evidence fixed. **Prospective DEV stress test:** for each grouped source, compare the target question alone, target first/last among related questions, and target first/last among unrelated questions. Separately hold the question fixed while moving its relevant evidence to early/middle/late positions in a bounded long state; record actual tokenizer counts and whether a native model rejects or truncates. Require the target's state, instructions, criteria and gold to stay byte-identical across the question-packing arm. Use paired label-flip and probability-drift rates, target accuracy, invalid/truncation, latency and throughput; cluster by original source, and report option-count and context-length bands. This tests a real deployment property without changing JevArena's frozen item counts or treating variants as independent questions.

## P1: Ranking utility, rare-event calibration and probability resolution

The [ORDER BY benchmark](https://github.com/yodablocks/jev-orderby-bench) explicitly separates Brier/ECE from pairwise inversion, AUC, Spearman/Kendall and top-k ties. Its 360-row topic task passed its gates while a separate 306-pair hard product-relevance probe failed four of six gates; neither result is a universal Jev score. [Rafe and Das](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24052v1) audited Jev on human-labelled crash factors and showed that two-decimal output resolution can impose a calibration floor for rare events; a conventional accuracy-based abstention target can also certify a useless always-negative screener. These are properties of that Jev version and task, **not assumed properties of Decision 2.0**.

For any natural human-labelled binary task with enough positives, add a non-headline diagnostic stratified by prevalence: AUPRC and positive-class F1 beside ROC-AUC, Brier/NLL/ECE, within-task pairwise inversion, top-k tie mass, and precision versus the actual human-review budget among flagged records. Count positives and independent source units; with fewer than a preregistered minimum, report descriptive estimates and no fitted per-task calibration. Inspect our own returned-probability granularity before applying any grid-floor analysis. Keep ranking and threshold utility as deployment-specific appendices rather than redefining the six-axis scalar. Any pairwise ranking loss should be tested only after a fixed-data CE/Brier control and evaluated for typed Score/Choice, CSS transfer and calibration regressions.

## P1: Multilingual state versus rubric language

Our existing XNLI/PAWS-X paired pilot changes state **and** question/rubric language together, and it contains no natural human-rated Score case. It correctly detects some language gaps, but cannot attribute them to evidence comprehension versus rubric parsing. The [small-decision-model cross-domain study](https://github.com/SnackTerminator/small-decision-model-cross-domain-degradation) tested a 2×2 state-language × criteria-language crossing on parallel NLI and reported an interaction; its own report notes non-pinned model revisions and incomplete cross-architecture causal separation, so its effect sizes should not be transferred to our family.

Use a bounded, prospective 2×2 on the *already pinned* parallel DEV source IDs: original-language state/English criterion, English state/original-language criterion, both English and both original-language, with semantic option mappings frozen and a bilingual review of translated criterion equivalence. Pair and bootstrap by original source ID, reporting each type/language cell and interaction rather than pooling translations. Add independently rated ordinal material only after rights and native-language review. This discriminates whether multilingual training should target state reading, criterion binding, or both; it does not by itself justify a new release rank.

## Do not infer a generic cascade advantage

Two primary studies reach different workload-specific conclusions. [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26550v1) report that a frozen confidence-gated Jev→stronger-judge cascade retains roughly 99% of the comparator's accuracy on their preference/factuality mix at lower fee; performance deteriorates on elaborate wrong answers and reference-free prose. [Rao and Callison-Burch](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29769v1) find that correlated confident errors in rubric judging leave at most a 1.5-point cross-fitted gain over the best single judge, and most panels lose accuracy. These are not contradictory measurements of the same task. A Decision 2.0 deployment cascade is an optional later product experiment: before claiming its value, freeze a task-specific threshold on separate calibration data, pair both judges on independent human gold, measure **both** first-stage confidence discrimination and conditional error complementarity, and compare matched quality/cost/latency with the stronger judge alone. No such cascade belongs in the standalone model-family rank.

## Priority and release boundary

1. Preserve the running matched 27B Score experiment and existing 4B multilingual/transfer receipts. Do not retrain or reopen labels for this audit.
2. Add the ordinal-human and request-shape diagnostics prospectively on DEV, using the fixed model revisions and native adapters; these have the best chance to change the next backbone/readout or data decision.
3. Add rare-event ranking and crossed-language diagnostics where independently labelled source groups and review capacity support them. These are supporting axes and should show negative results as well as wins.
4. Do not import author-reported scores from the live catalog into our rank, assume two-decimal Jev resolution applies to our weights, assign a causal explanation to shared human-rubric offsets, or alter the preregistered six-axis JevArena formula after inspecting model outcomes. The independent authored release pool and same-panel FINAL gates remain necessary before model publication.
